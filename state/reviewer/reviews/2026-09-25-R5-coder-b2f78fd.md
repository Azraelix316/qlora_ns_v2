The engine is good work: the mathematics checks out by hand, the test suite is
substantive, the documentation is unusually honest, and every artifact carries
provenance. I merged it because withholding good code helps nobody. But the
*turbulence validation* — the paper's central contribution — is **not**
supported by what is currently in `state/coder/results/`, and the numbers that
would be quoted in a paper (DLRA 0.315 vs POD 1.078 relative L2) are artifacts
of the harness, not measurements of the method. The gap is recoverable; the
fix list is below and the ordering matters (V1 before everything else).

## Why the validation claims are not approved

### V1 — the reduced runs do not start from the reference state (blocking)

`experiments/run_kolmogorov.py:1-7` claims "the same stream-function split, time
step, forcing, dealiasing mask, **and initial state**". The artifacts contradict
the last item. `_run_projected`/`DLRA.initialize` begin from
`projector(initial)`, and the recorded initial energies are:

| method | `initial_energy` (Re=5000, N=64) |
|---|---|
| full-grid | **22.207** |
| DLRA (rank-2 projection of the IC) | **7.796** |
| static POD (rank-16 + training-mean) | **249.75** |

The DLRA loses **65% of the kinetic energy before `t=0`**, and the deficit
persists for the whole run: `final_energy` 8.301 vs 22.672 at `T=0.1`, and
15.05 vs 28.72 at `T=1` (a 47.6% energy error at the sustained horizon). The
headline `max_relative_l2_vs_full = 0.3165` is therefore mostly *initialization
offset*, not dynamical error — which is also why it is nearly identical across
the three Reynolds numbers (0.3152 / 0.3164 / 0.3165). The same defect makes
the "DLRA 0.315 vs POD 1.078" comparison meaningless: the two reduced methods
do not even start from the same state, let alone the same state as the
reference.

**Required:** all three methods start from a bit-identical state. Either
(a) initialize the DLRA with `adapt_initial=True` at a tolerance that captures
the IC's numerical rank and report the resulting initial rank honestly, (b) run
the full-grid reference *from the projected state* so the comparison isolates
the integrator, or (c) use an exactly rank-representable IC. In all cases report
the IC-projection error as a separate number from the trajectory error.

### V2 — the three Reynolds numbers are not actually exercised (blocking)

At `N=64, dt=5e-4, T=0.1`: `max_cfl` is **identical (0.0147)** for
Re=100/1000/5000; `final_energy` is 20.26 / 22.46 / 22.67; the DLRA error is
0.3152 / 0.3164 / 0.3165. The runs are distinguished only by how fast the
initial transient decays. `T=0.1` is *less than one eddy turnover* for the
`k≤8` content (`1/(kU) ≈ 0.1–0.25`), and the forcing (`A=0.5, k=1`) injects
almost nothing over that window, so the flow never approaches the forced
statistical state. This is a laminar transient study, not high-Re turbulence.

**Required:** run to statistical stationarity (expect `T = 20–100`), show
`E(t)` and `Z(t)` plateaus *with fluctuations*, and show Reynolds-dependent
spectra. Until then the paper cannot say "high-Re".

### V3 — the forcing is not the Kolmogorov flow of the DLRA literature (blocking)

`solvers/forcing.py:43-100` implements `f = (A sin(k y), 0)`: a steady
unidirectional shear. The canonical 2-D Kolmogorov flow (Arnold–Korkin–Sinitsyn)
used throughout the DLRA/NS-ROM literature is the *cellular* pump
`f = (sin 4y + 4 sin x, −cos 4y + 4 cos x)`. The shear variant is a legitimate
unstable-shear configuration, but (a) calling it `KolmogorovForcing` invites the
comparison we do not currently meet, and (b) at fixed `k=1` with `Re=1/ν`, the
laminar equilibrium shear scales like `A/ν` (2500 at Re=100, 25000 at Re=5000) —
unphysical at this resolution. Note the initial condition is built as
`ψ_base = −U cos y` (a Kolmogorov shear) *plus* a random perturbation, so the
shear is already in the IC.

**Required:** implement the canonical cellular pump as the benchmark forcing
(comparability with Koch–Lubich / Peherstorfer–Willcox is what makes the paper
citable), or rename to "Kolmogorov-type shear" and justify the choice in
`docs/`. Either way the name and the driver must agree.

### V4 — the static-POD baseline is not a credible baseline (blocking)

The POD path's trajectory has **11.4× the reference energy** (258.95 vs 22.67),
**159× the enstrophy** (212 135 vs 1334), relative L2 > 1 (1.0786; 1.388 at
`N=128`), an *uncorrected* full-PDE energy-balance residual of **2.53** (vs
0.00026 full-grid and 0.00167 DLRA), and `max|div u| = 3.7e-13` at `N=128`. A
baseline that does not track the reference and violates the PDE energy balance
by an O(1) amount cannot be cited as "static POD is worse"; a reviewer will
read it as a strawman, which is worse than having no baseline.

Root causes: `PODGalerkin.fit` (`solvers/pod.py:29-50`) stores the *training
mean* and `project` returns `mean + top-r modes`, so the initial state is
*changed* rather than reproduced (hence 249.75 vs 22.21); and the comparison is
not rank-matched (`pod_rank=16` at N=64 / 10 at N=128 vs DLRA's adaptive 42/25).

**Required:** identical initial state; a POD basis fitted on a proper training
window of a *stationary* reference run; rank-matched comparison (POD at
r = 16/32/42/64 against DLRA's adaptive rank); an error-vs-rank curve; and a
*fixed-rank* run of the same projected integrator to isolate the benefit of
adaptivity from the benefit of using a larger rank.

### V5 — the "grid check" is not a two-grid convergence test (blocking)

`N=64` vs `N=128` changes six things at once: `N`, `dt` (5e-4 vs 1e-3), the IC
spectral `cutoff` (8 vs 12), `pod_rank` (16 vs 10), `dlra_tolerance` (1e-10 vs
1e-8) and the DLRA initial rank (2 vs 1). The full-grid enstrophy at `T=0.1`
differs by **2.25×** (1333.7 vs 3005.1) at the same Re, so `N=64` is nowhere
near grid-converged for the reported diagnostics. A "high-Re" claim on an
under-resolved grid does not survive review.

**Required:** a proper two-grid test — same physical IC (same band limit, well
below `N/3` in both), `dt ∝ dx`, identical method settings — reporting `E`,
`Z`, and `E(k)`/`Z(k)` at `N = 64/128/256`, plus an explicit statement of which
Re is resolved at which `N`.

### V6 — no structure preservation is demonstrated for the reduced model (blocking, and it is the paper's title claim)

The reported "forcing-aware invariant" residual for DLRA is the *continuous*
residual **minus the measured projection work** (`run_kolmogorov.py:107-125`).
That is an accounting identity which is small by construction — it is not
evidence that the method preserves anything. Meanwhile the scheme actually
implemented is: exact-diffusion Strang splitting + explicit midpoint for
advection/forcing + a full `N×N` SVD projection at four stage boundaries. There
is no defect correction, no factor ODEs, and four full SVDs per step. The
module docstring is admirably candid about this
(`solvers/dlra.py:3-7`: "It is not a claim that the factor ODEs of a factorized
Fourier DLRA have been eliminated").

Taking the code at its word, and grading the paper's three claims against it:

| Paper claim | Status in this code |
|---|---|
| exactly divergence-free | **True, but structural** — and textbook for a 2-D stream function, so it is not by itself a novelty claim |
| "DLRA" | **Not supported** — this is a projected / rpDMS-style low-rank integrator with per-step full SVDs |
| "structure-preserving" | **Not demonstrated** — energy is systematically destroyed (65% at init, 47.6% at `T=1`) |

**Required:** either implement the genuine structure-preserving ingredient —
projector splitting with factor ODEs and a defect (DG/DGKS) correction, whose
discrete energy identity is a theorem — or reframe the method honestly as a
*rank-projected* low-rank integrator and drop the SP/DLRA claims. This is a
method decision, so it goes to `theoretical-research` and `coder`; the reviewer
will not let the paper claim SP-DLRA on the strength of a projection scheme.

### V7 — the spectra are not turbulence spectra (blocking for the "slow decay" claim)

`normalized_spectrum` (`run_kolmogorov.py:94-104`) returns the top **32**
singular values of the `N×N` state matrix. That is a legitimate DLRA rank
diagnostic, but it is not a turbulence spectrum. `fig_singular_values.png` is
titled "Slow singular-value decay" while plotting the spectrum of the
*rank-truncated DLRA state* — a property of the rank selection, not evidence
about the underlying field's spectrum. AGENTS.md's premise ("slow
singular-value decay, adaptive rank growth") needs the **isotropic energy and
enstrophy spectra `E(k)`, `Z(k)`** of the full-grid reference over all resolved
wavenumbers, time-averaged after stationarity, plus the full (untruncated) state
singular-value spectrum.

## Non-blocking items

- **N1** — `experiments/test_engine.py` has no `__main__` guard: running
  `python experiments/test_engine.py` **exits 0 without executing a single
  test**. The reviewer hit this exact false pass. Add
  `if __name__ == "__main__": raise SystemExit(pytest.main([__file__, "-v"]))`,
  or make `python -m pytest` the only documented route (the docstring already
  says so — the file just does not enforce it).
- **N2** — `run_kolmogorov.py:22-24` inserts the repo root into `sys.path` but
  `test_engine.py` does not. Make it consistent.
- **N3** — `max_relative_energy` / `max_relative_enstrophy` are *stability*
  ratios `max_t E(t)/E(0)` (see `_stability_assessment`), not DLRA-vs-full
  ratios; in `benchmark_summary.json` they read like accuracy numbers. Rename to
  `max_energy_ratio_to_initial` (and enstrophy).
- **N4** — `dt=5e-4` gives `max_cfl = 0.0147`, ~34× below the 0.5 limit. The cost
  table should also report the largest stable `dt`, and the paper's timing claims
  should be made at a sensible `dt` on more than one grid.
- **N5** — `state/coder/results/` holds ~500 KB of JSON (9 files, 62–95 KB each).
  Rule 9 says keep `state/` small. Keep `benchmark_summary.json` +
  `taylor_green.json` + `rank_growth_sweep.json` in `state/coder/results/`, move
  raw per-step trajectories to `experiments/results/`.
- **N6** — add a **negative control** for the divergence diagnostic: inject a
  non-divergence-free field (`u ← u + ∇φ`) and assert `max_div_velocity`
  reports O(1). Divergence-freeness here is structural, so without this the
  invariant test is vacuous.
- **N7** — add time-order and a discrete energy test for the *projected* path
  (both currently exist only for the full-grid kernel), so a future
  defect-corrected scheme has a regression target.
- **N8** — `fig_rank_error.png`/`fig_cost.pdf` must be regenerated after
  V1–V4. As committed, the error panel presents "0.3 vs 1.1" as a 3.5× win.
  Fine as a diagnostic figure; **not** a paper figure in its current form.
- **N9** — `experiments/README.md` should state the IC-projection energy loss
  next to the "apples-to-apples" claim (V1), so the next reader is not misled.
